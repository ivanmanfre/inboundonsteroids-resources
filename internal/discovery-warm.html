Discovery — Warm Referral v1
← Cold view
Phase 0 · Pre-call 2 min

Context (silent, before dialing)

Write down silently — never reveal you have this intel:

  • Who referred them + what the referrer said about their business
  • What they likely came for (booking form, referrer notes) — LMs, content, automation, audit
  • Partner alignment — do they have a co-founder, is that person joining?
Critical rule
Treat them as if you know nothing about their business. Ask open questions that elicit the same info the referrer already gave you. Never say "Kyle mentioned X" — that breaks the referral pipeline.
Phase 1 · Open + context confirm 2-3 min

Open + context confirm

"Hey, thanks for hopping on. [Referrer] mentioned you're looking at [thing they came for]. Before I pitch anything, I want to make sure I understand the business and where you actually feel friction. I'll keep this to ~30 min."

Then 2-3 open questions to validate context (you already know — they need to surface in their words):

  • "Tell me about [their business] — who you sell to, what you sell, where revenue comes from."
  • "What are you currently doing for [content / LMs / outbound]? How does it get produced today?"
  • "If you had to name one thing you keep saying 'we should do that' and never get to — what comes up first?"

Listen for their actual pain in their words, what they prioritize, what they're embarrassed about. Referrer intel maps to ~70% — the other 30% is your edge.

Phase 2 · Pain + bottleneck + capacity 8-10 min

Pain + capacity test

"Where's the bottleneck in your week right now — sales, delivery, ops, content?"

Labels to deploy (pattern-interrupt)

  • "Everything goes through me" → "Sounds like you ARE the business then."
  • "Team handles delivery but I touch sales" → "3 days a week not growing the business."
  • "We have systems but inconsistent" → "Works at $X but not $Y. So it breaks at scale."

Capacity test (the big one)

"If someone sent you 5 perfect-fit clients tomorrow, could you actually take them on? Or would it just make things worse?"

  • "We'd manage" → "How? What breaks first?" → forces them to name the bottleneck
  • "No, we'd break" → that IS the bottleneck. Anchor everything around solving it.

Quantify (only if pain is time/cost)

"Roughly how many hours per week is [pain] eating? At [their hourly rate or founder time value]? Over 12 months that's [$ amount]. Sound right?"

Save vs Gain (you infer from their answers, don't ask separately)

  • TIME/EFFICIENCY pain → Save → "What would you do with [X hours] back?"
  • OUTPUT/CEILING pain → Gain → "If the ceiling moved to [Y], what changes?"
Phase 3 · Trigger event 2 min · do not skip

Why now?

88% of closes have a trigger event

"Why are you looking at this now versus 6 months ago?"

Probe for triggers

  • New hire that didn't deliver
  • Lost a client
  • Competitor shipped something
  • Hit revenue plateau
  • Partner pressure ("[partner] keeps asking when we're going to fix X")
  • AI-curious moment

If "later" / vague

"When's the last time things calmed down for more than a week?"

"The thing you're waiting to finish — what is it? After that, what's next?"

"Founders who wait for the right time are still waiting two years later. The work happens while it's busy."

Hard DQ (Kyle's language — use when they keep resisting)

"You're not ready. Call me when the pain beats the excuse." Walks away. Protects your time + perceived value.

Phase 4 · Quantified summary 2 min

Recap + screen-share

Recap in their words:

"So you're [pain in their words], you want [outcome in their words], trigger is [trigger]. What did I miss?"

Wait for them to add or correct. Then:

"Cool. Mind if I share my screen?"

The verbal pitch

"Two options for you guys.

Option 1 — Lead magnet + content system, around $7,500. You get the lead magnet engine, content generation system, branded resource pages, email capture, and 1 month of post-launch support. End of engagement at Day 45 — hands-off after that. That's it.

Option 2 — Fractional Month 1, $10,000. Everything in Option 1, plus three things that change the math:

1. Custom operations panel — you see all the metrics of your lead magnets and LinkedIn content in one place. Interactions, comments, engagement, conversions. But more importantly, it works as the base for any future systems I build for you — every workflow I ship plugs into the same panel, so you have ONE surface to see everything running.

2. Fully built newsletter system — campaigns + automations on ConvertKit, with ideas drafting baked in so you're not staring at a blank page each week.

3. Free 90-Day Blueprint audit + implementation plan. I audit your full business — content, outbound, production, partnerships — and deliver a 90-day roadmap. Based on that, I propose next months' plans. Could be the $6,500 Active build tier. Could be nothing if there's not enough to justify it. The audit is yours regardless of whether you continue.

For $2,500 more you get all three. It's a no-brainer if there's anything beyond the lead magnet that's worth building — and the audit tells us exactly what's worth building."

Honesty anchor (the brand differentiator — don't skip)
"And I want to flag — based on the audit, I might tell you straight that there's not enough to justify continuation. You'd graduate to a Care Plan at $1k/mo for ongoing maintenance, or just walk. I'd rather lose a month of revenue than ship a padded roadmap."
Pace-to-absorption
"If we end up continuing past m1, I pace to your team's absorption — not my delivery speed. Each month we ship only what you can actually integrate."
Phase 6 · Close 3 min

Close + onboarding sequence

"Want to start with Option 1 or Option 2? Standard onboarding either way:

• Discovery done today (we're here now)
• You get the intake link in the next 24 hours — ~20 min conversation with an AI agent that walks you through your business
• Kickoff + audit walkthrough call within the first week
• Builds start shipping in days
• 90-Day Plan Review at week 3-4 — if Option 2, this is when we look at the audit findings together and decide on the next plan. No surprises, it's already on the calendar from day one.

Want me to send the engagement letter + payment link?"

If "let me think"

"Totally fair. What specifically? Sometimes I can answer it right now. The audit (Option 2) is yours regardless — only commitment is the m1 build, which I refund if I don't deliver."

If "let me discuss with [partner]"

"Cool. What's your decision process from here? When can you get [partner] on a 20-min call so I can walk them through what we discussed?"

"Let's lock the follow-up call now — what's [day] look like?"

Watch For

Failure modes (warm-specific)

  1. Don't run full cold discovery. Warm leads bought trust from the referrer. Don't re-establish it for 45 min — close in 30. If you find yourself going through cold script's Phases 1-10, stop. You're over-pitching.
  2. Never reveal referrer intel. "Kyle mentioned X" breaks the referral pipeline. Ask open questions that surface the same info in their words.
  3. Match the pitch to what they came for. If they booked because they want LMs, lead with LM scope. Don't pitch the kitchen sink.
  4. $2.5k delta math is the close lever. "For $2.5k more you get panel + newsletter + audit." Make it explicit. Don't bury it.
  5. Audit is the deliverable, not the bait. "Yours regardless of whether you continue." Never promise it will find m2-3 work. Promise it will be honest. The honesty IS the brand.
  6. Don't auto-discount on pushback. $7.5k single firm. $10k m1 firm. $6.5k m2-3 firm. Single-project IS the downsell — don't drop the $10k rate.
  7. Operations panel is content/automation results, NOT Kyle's lane. Safe to mention for Kyle referrals. It's a results dashboard for systems YOU build, not ops/management/playbook infra.
  8. Lock follow-up on the call. Never end with "I'll email you to schedule." If they need to think, schedule the follow-up before hanging up.
